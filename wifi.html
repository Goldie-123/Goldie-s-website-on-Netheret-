Wi-Fi and Internet Troubleshooting Guide

Experiencing Wi-Fi or internet connection problems? This guide provides simple instructions to help you identify and solve common connection issues.

First, check whether the problem affects one device or all the devices connected to your network.

1. Identify the Problem

Check which situation describes your problem:

Only one device cannot connect

The problem may be with the device's Wi-Fi settings or network adapter.

All devices are offline

The problem may be with your router, modem or internet service provider.

Connected but no internet

Your device may be connected to the router, but the router may not have an internet connection.

2. Quick Fixes to Try First

  1. Turn Wi-Fi off and on: Switch Wi-Fi off, wait 10 seconds and turn it back on.
  2. Restart your device: Restart your phone, tablet or computer to clear temporary connection problems.
  3. Restart your router and modem: Unplug both devices for approximately 30 seconds. Reconnect the modem first, wait for it to start, then reconnect the router.
  4. Check for service outages: Visit your internet provider's website to check whether there is a service interruption.

3. Troubleshooting One Device

If other devices can connect to the internet, follow the instructions for the device experiencing the problem.

iPhone and iPad
  1. Open Settings and select Wi-Fi.
  2. Select your network and choose Forget This Network.
  3. Reconnect using the correct Wi-Fi password.
  4. Check for available software updates.
  5. If necessary, consider resetting network settings.
Android Phones and Tablets
  1. Open Settings and select Network and Internet.
  2. Select your Wi-Fi network.
  3. Choose Forget and reconnect to the network.
  4. Restart your device if the problem continues.
  5. Check for available system updates.
Windows Computers
  1. Open Windows Settings.
  2. Select Network and Internet.
  3. Run the available network troubleshooter.
  4. Check that your Wi-Fi adapter is enabled.
  5. Check Device Manager for Wi-Fi driver updates.
  6. Restart the computer and test the connection.
Mac Computers
  1. Open System Settings and select Wi-Fi.
  2. Disconnect and reconnect to your network.
  3. Forget the network and reconnect if necessary.
  4. Use Wireless Diagnostics if the problem continues.
  5. Check for available macOS updates.
Smart TVs and Gaming Consoles
  1. Restart the device.
  2. Check its Wi-Fi connection settings.
  3. Forget the network and reconnect.
  4. Check for software or firmware updates.
  5. Check whether the device supports your router's Wi-Fi band.

4. Wi-Fi Not Working on Any Device

If every device has lost its connection, check your router and modem.

  1. Check that both devices have power.
  2. Check that all cables are securely connected.
  3. Look at the internet and warning lights on your router.
  4. Restart the modem and router.
  5. Check whether your internet provider has an outage.
  6. Contact your provider if the connection does not return.

5. Common Wi-Fi Problems

Weak Wi-Fi Signal

Place your router in a central, elevated position. Avoid placing it behind furniture or near thick walls.

Slow Internet Speed

Disconnect devices that are not being used. Check your internet package and test your connection using an Ethernet cable if possible.

Wi-Fi Interference

Other wireless devices and nearby networks can interfere with your Wi-Fi signal. Try changing the router's wireless channel or using another supported Wi-Fi band.

Router Overheating

Place your router in an open and ventilated area. Keep its ventilation openings clear.

Outdated Router Software

Check the router manufacturer's instructions for available firmware updates.

6. When to Contact Your Internet Provider

Contact your Internet Service Provider (ISP) if:

  • Your internet connection is still unavailable.
  • Your router shows an internet connection warning.
  • There is a reported service outage.
  • Your connection repeatedly disconnects.

7. Important Safety Advice

Do not open electrical equipment or attempt to repair internal router components. Be careful when changing network settings. A network reset may remove saved passwords and other connection settings.
